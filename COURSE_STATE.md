## Content already taught in Block 1.1A
- Research topic (a broad area) vs focused/answerable research question.
- A research question can specify population, exposure (when applicable), outcome and research aim; descriptive questions may have no exposure.
- Example independent of personal projects: habitual sleep duration and systolic blood pressure among working adults.
- The question helps determine the target quantity, evidence needs, design and analysis.

## Content newly taught in Block 1.1B
- Four common, non-exhaustive and overlapping research aims: descriptive, associational, causal, predictive.
- One coherent general example domain: physical activity and hypertension.
- Descriptive: quantify frequency/distribution; association: quantify observed relationships; causal: contrasts between intervention conditions; prediction: performance when estimating outcomes.
- Distinguish research aim from design and statistical method; an association or strong predictor does not by itself prove causation.
