# Course Syllabus & Knowledge Dependencies

**Core curriculum v1.0 · Foundations of Epidemiology & Biostatistics**

**Overall reasoning sequence:** Research question → study design → population & sampling → units, variables & measurement → descriptive distribution → statistical inference → association measures & models → validity & causation → critical appraisal & reporting.

Crosscutting topics (ethics, bias, missing data and generalizability) appear whenever needed; a later dedicated chapter deepens them. The nine chapters provide a **foundation**, not a claim to exhaust every specialty method.

## 01 · Research Questions & Study Designs
### 1.1 Understanding Research Questions
- **1.1A** Topic vs research question; population, exposure (when relevant), outcome, aim — **learner confirmed understanding 2026-10-28**
- **1.1B** Four common research aims: descriptive, associational, causal, predictive (non-exhaustive, overlapping) — **delivered; revision needed after learner feedback**
- **1.1C** Focused/answerable/feasible questions; PICO/PECO, time, operationalization and FINER — **delivered; learner elected to continue; no mastery claim**
- **1.1D** Bridge from research question to estimand, estimator/estimate, evidence requirements and design — **formally taught; pending learner review**
### 1.2 Study Designs
- Observational vs interventional; time and direction of observation; no false single-axis hierarchy
- Descriptive designs: case reports/series, descriptive cross-sectional and ecological studies
- Analytic observational designs: cross-sectional, case–control, cohort; important nested variations
- Experimental/quasi-experimental designs: randomized and nonrandomized; randomization unit and intervention structure
- Compare designs by participant selection, timing, estimands, strengths and limitations

## 02 · Populations & Sampling
- Population, target/source/accessible/eligible populations and analysis samples
- Sampling unit, observational unit, unit of analysis and clustering
- Sampling frame, coverage, nonresponse and selection mechanisms
- **Probability**: simple random, systematic, stratified, cluster, multistage — all explained, with relevant design implications
- **Nonprobability**: convenience, purposive, quota, snowball and consecutive sampling — definitions, differences, overlap and classification caveats
- Sampling vs random assignment; generalizability, representativeness and introductory sample size

## 03 · Variables, Measurement & Data Structure
- Unit → variable → value → record → dataset
- Quantitative/categorical, continuous/discrete, nominal/ordinal, measurement scales
- Exposure/predictor/outcome/covariate; context-specific role
- Operational definitions, reliability, validity, measurement error and misclassification
- Derived/categorized variables, composite scores, missingness and documentation

## 04 · Descriptive Epidemiology, Statistics & Distributions
- Prevalence, cumulative incidence/risk, incidence rates, person-time, denominators
- Person/place/time distributions; descriptive tables and figures
- Mean, median, mode; variance, SD, range, IQR; proportion; skewness and outliers
- Normal distribution, standardization, z scores and normality caveats

## 05 · Probability & Statistical Inference
- Probability, parameters vs statistics, point estimators and estimates
- Sampling distributions; SE vs SD; assumptions for inference
- Confidence intervals; null hypotheses and P-values; Type I/II errors; power
- Design-based vs model-based inference and scope of population claims

## 06 · Association Measures, Statistical Tests & Model Selection
- Risk difference, risk ratio, odds ratio, prevalence ratio, incidence rate ratio; meaningful comparison scales
- Independent vs paired observations and outcome types
- t test (Welch, paired), ANOVA, chi-square, Fisher exact, nonparametric alternatives and correlation
- Test assumptions, effect sizes, CIs, multiplicity and principled selection

## 07 · Regression Modeling & Interpretation
- Conditional relationships and regression model meaning
- Linear and logistic models, Poisson/modified Poisson and survival analysis introduction
- Coefficients, coding, nonlinear terms, transformations, interactions, diagnostics
- Clustering, missingness, robust SE, sensitivity analysis, overfitting vs overadjustment

## 08 · Bias, Confounding & Causal Inference
- Random variability, bias, precision, internal/external validity
- Selection, information/measurement bias, confounding
- Confounder, mediator, collider, effect modifier, DAG and adjustment sets
- Temporality, reverse causation, exchangeability, positivity, consistency, causal estimands and appropriate claims

## 09 · Critical Appraisal & Research Reporting
- Evaluate full methods-to-conclusions reasoning of published research
- Reproducibility, transparent reporting, research ethics and communication
- Introduction to screening/diagnostic measures, surveillance and evidence synthesis as applications (extended modules available)
- Explain and defend findings in academic English; turn reviewed knowledge into teaching scripts

## Optional extension tracks (only when deliberately scheduled)
Survey weighting and complex designs; advanced longitudinal, multilevel, survival and causal models; screening and diagnostic test evaluation; surveillance/outbreak investigations; evidence synthesis/meta-analysis; psychometrics; R pipelines.

## Quality principle
Each lesson is broken into manageable blocks with **complete scope and all essential branches explained**. Examples support transferable knowledge; the student's T2DM or OSA projects do not define the course. A block is not considered mastered just because teaching text was sent.

## Numbering note
Older materials used “Lesson 1.2 — Population, Sampling, and Statistical Inference.” That numbering is **superseded**: the current Lesson 1.2 covers Study Designs and Chapter 02 covers Populations & Sampling.
